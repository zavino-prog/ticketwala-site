## Findings and implementation

1. Brand consistency: existing Facebook art differs from the client's supplied mark. The site uses the supplied TicketWala logo and its navy/blue/orange palette. Updating the Facebook profile and cover would help the handoff feel consistent; no Facebook changes were made.
2. Service clarity: the cover gives five service categories but little explanation. The site explains four main services in a compact grid and provides a separate airport-services enquiry.
3. Conversion path: each main service and destination has a direct WhatsApp draft relevant to that enquiry. Facebook remains the secondary contact channel. Mobile visitors have a persistent enquiry button.
4. Credibility: address, email, phone, and the exact company page are visible. No invented ratings, testimonials, customer counts, airline partnerships, certifications, response times, or price guarantees.
5. Travel inspiration: Thailand, Maldives, and Dubai are destination imagery and enquiry prompts, not verified live offers. A visible note says availability and packages are confirmed when enquiring. No invented prices or departure dates.
6. Booking expectations: FAQs explain that this is an enquiry website and quotes, terms, and payments are confirmed directly. The process encourages reviewing inclusions and terms before booking. Visa copy does not promise approval.
7. Performance: compressed local photos instead of embedded Facebook feeds, local font instead of external font requests, no video, no frontend framework, and optional deferred animation.

## Remaining business opportunities

- Replace old Facebook profile/cover branding and update the domain to ticketwalabd.com.
- Publish real, permission-cleared customer reviews and trip imagery before adding testimonial sections.
- Supply current offers, any verified credentials, and a documented cancellation policy if those should appear later.
- Full historical page/post audit requires access to material that Facebook did not expose during this session.
